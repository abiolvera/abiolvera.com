Abi Olvera

I write Positive Sum, a newsletter about why things cost so much, why institutions make mistakes, and what AI is actually changing.

I also run DC Abundance, a community exploring why it’s so hard to build housing, energy, and infrastructure in America.

Before this, I spent more than a decade as a U.S. diplomat.

Background

I work as a generalist. My writing has appeared in Asterisk, the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists, where I was an editorial fellow, and the Golden Gate Institute for AI. I also wrote The Underdog’s Guide to Scholarships, a book for low-income students, and gave a TEDx talk on traffic deaths.

Some of my writing is about class and cultural capital. Richer and poorer Americans mostly live in separate social worlds, which makes it easy for policy to miss the people at the bottom.

I started at community college and went on to the University of Richmond and Yale. At the State Department, including postings in Senegal and Egypt, I worked on national security, biosecurity, emerging technology, and China, as well as macroeconomics, poverty, and crisis preparedness.
